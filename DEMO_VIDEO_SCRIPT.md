# HIMADRI: 7-Minute Demo Video Script
**Team Who Let the Bugs Out · SIH 2026 · PS 26060**

| Block | Time | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Cold open (hook) | 0:00–0:35 | 35s |
| Title + team | 0:35–0:50 | 15s |
| Problem statement | 0:50–1:35 | 45s |
| Meet HIMADRI | 1:35–2:00 | 25s |
| **Demo: software twin** | 2:00–3:05 | 65s |
| **Demo: PolarTwin hardware** ⭐ | 3:05–4:35 | 90s |
| **Demo: break it, cut it, save it** ⭐ | 4:35–6:35 | 120s |
| **Demo: agent + QR** | 6:35–6:50 | 15s |
| Close | 6:50–7:00 | 10s |

**Delivery:** about 140 words a minute. Short sentences. Leave space for the sound design; the pauses matter as much as the lines.
**Key:** 🎬 = what's on screen · 🔊 = audio/SFX · 🎙️ = voiceover

🎬 Pure black. After a second, white text fades in slowly: **−40°C**

🎙️ *(low, slow, almost a whisper)* "Minus forty degrees. Winds that can hit two hundred miles an hour."

🎬 Glitch cut. Text slams in: **30 kbps**
🔊 A 2-second burst of a 90s dial-up modem screech, then a hard digital glitch cut.
🎙️ "And an internet connection slower than a dial-up modem from 1998."

**0:14**
🎬 Slow drone shot or still of an Antarctic station in a blizzard, desaturated.
🔊 The wind comes back. Faint radio static and a crackly, unintelligible voice.
🎙️ "For months, no ship can reach you. No plane can land. If something breaks..." *(beat)* "...you're on your own."

**0:22**
🔊 **Total silence.** Cut everything.
🎙️ *(deadpan)* "So how do you check the fuel that's keeping twenty-five people alive?"

🔊 Record scratch, or a comedic single-note "bwomp".

🔊 Thumbtack "tk" sound.
🎙️ "The duty roster? Paper. Pinned to a wall."

🎬 Black. Text types on letter by letter: *This is how India runs Antarctica.*
🔊 A riser starts climbing.
🎙️ "This is how India runs Antarctica." *(beat)* "Not anymore."

## PART 2: DEMO (2:00–7:00)

> **Recording setup:** Screen-record the app at 1080p or higher. For the hardware section, use a **split screen**: an overhead phone camera on the PolarTwin board on the left, the 3D twin on the right. Put a mic near the board so the **real buzzer is audible**; that sound is the payoff.

### 2:00–2:25 · COMMAND CENTER

🎬 The Maitri overview dashboard with live numbers moving: fuel endurance, power, food, weather, open alerts.
🔊 The music settles into a steady driving pulse. Soft UI ticks as the numbers update.
🎙️ "This is the command center. Every subsystem in one view: fuel endurance, power, food, waste, weather, open alerts. None of this is a screenshot. The telemetry streams in live, second by second."

🎬 Hover over one number to show its unit and age label.
🎙️ "Every number shows its unit and how old it is. In Antarctica, stale data can kill you."

### 2:25–3:05 · THE 3D TWIN

🎬 Open the **Bharati 3D twin**. Orbit around the station, then **explode the floors** apart.
🔊 A mechanical "shhk-shhk" as each floor separates, with a deep whoosh.
🎙️ "Bharati. A hundred and thirty-four containers, four levels, rebuilt in 3D."

🎬 Click the **CHP room**. The detail panel slides in with assets and live states.
🔊 A crisp click, then a panel slide.
🎙️ "Click any room and you see what's inside it and how it's doing, right now."

🎬 Switch to the **Thermal overlay**. The station floods with heat colors.
🔊 A warm swell of sound.
🎙️ "And this overlay isn't decoration. It's the same live telemetry, mapped onto the building. You don't read a spreadsheet. You **see** where the station is hurting."

### 3:05–4:35 · POLARTWIN: THE HARDWARE TWIN ⭐

🔊 **The music cuts out.** Only a quiet ambient hum remains.
🎬 Switch to split screen: the physical board on the left, the **Maitri 3D twin** on the right.
🎙️ "But a twin is only as real as the data flowing into it. So we built one."

**3:12 · The build**
🎬 Slow pan across the board. Callout labels pop onto each component as it's named.
🎙️ "This is **PolarTwin**. An Arduino reads the sensors, and a Raspberry Pi gateway streams them into HIMADRI. There are three rooms, just like the station."
🎬 Callouts: **Room 1: Environment** (DHT11 temp/humidity, MQ-2 gas) · **Room 2: Safety & Occupancy** (IR presence, ultrasonic distance, Hall-effect door sensor) · **Room 3: Machine Health** (ADXL335 accelerometer)
🎙️ "Environment: temperature, humidity, gas. Safety: presence, distance, door. And machine health, with an accelerometer on the equipment."

**3:30 · Beat 1: gas leak**
🎬 Hold an **unlit** butane lighter near the MQ-2 and release a little gas. On the right, **Room 1 turns red** in the 3D twin. On the board, the RGB LED switches from green to **red**.
🔊 A rising tension tone as the value climbs, then an alarm sting when it crosses the threshold.
🎙️ "Watch. A gas leak, right here, on the real sensor." *(beat)* "...And the twin sees it. Same second. Room 1 goes critical."

**3:48 · Beat 2: someone walks in**
🎬 Wave a hand past the IR sensor and open the "door" (magnet on the Hall sensor). Room 2 in the twin lights up: **Occupied**, **Door open**.
🔊 Two soft chimes.
🎙️ "Someone enters. The twin knows who's where. That matters when you're counting heads in a blizzard."

**3:58 · Beat 3: the machine shakes**
🎬 **Shake or tap the board** near the ADXL335. On the twin, Room 3's vibration value spikes and the **Failure Indicator flips HEALTHY → FAULT**.
🔊 A low rumble and a glitch hit.
🎙️ "A generator starts vibrating out of spec. The twin flags a fault before anyone on station even feels it."

**4:10 · Beat 4: the reverse direction (the WOW moment)**
🔊 **Silence.** Let it breathe for a second.
🎙️ *(quiet)* "Everything so far flows one way. Station to screen. Now watch this."
🎬 Close-up of the cursor in the 3D twin clicking **BUZZER: ON**.
🔊 **The real buzzer SCREAMS on the physical board.** Keep it raw and loud. Hold it for 1.5 seconds.
🎬 Click **OFF**. It stops instantly.
🎙️ "I clicked a button in a 3D model, and a real alarm went off in the real world. That command went through authentication, got queued, was picked up by the gateway, and was confirmed."

**4:28**
🎙️ "The station telemetry you saw earlier is simulated. **This isn't.** Real sensors, the real pipeline, and a digital twin that talks back."
🔊 **The music slams back in** at full energy.

### 4:35–5:05 · SOMETHING BREAKS

🎬 Back to the main app. Trigger **fuel_leak** in the simulator.
🔊 A drip sound, then a klaxon-style alert sting.
🎙️ "Back at Maitri. A fuel transfer line starts leaking."
🎬 The alert fires, the fuel endurance projection **drops**, and the tank **glows red** on the 3D twin.
🎙️ "Detected. Graded by severity. Pinned to its exact location on the twin. And if nobody acknowledges it, it escalates on a timer."

🎬 Open the alert, then click **Diagnose**.
🔊 A scanning "scan-beep-beep" sound.
🎙️ "Hit Diagnose: ranked probable causes, the evidence for each one, and the exact check sequence, based on the maintenance procedures the expedition teams actually follow."

### 5:05–5:45 · CUT THE LINK ⭐⭐ (THE MONEY SHOT)

🎙️ *(slower, heavier)* "Now it's winter. No ship. No aircraft. Let's do the thing that breaks every other digital twin."
🎬 Admin → **Cut Link**. The banner turns to **PNR-ISOLATED**.
🔊 **The music CUTS TO DEAD SILENCE.** Play one electrical snap, then just wind.
🎙️ *(over the wind)* "Link's dead."
*(beat)*
🎬 Keep clicking fast: dashboards still live, alerts still firing, a command still going through, forecasts still running.
🔊 A single low heartbeat drum under each click, slowly building back.
🎙️ "Dashboards: working. Alerts: working. Remote control: working. Forecasts: working." *(beat)* "**Nothing here needs India.** The station is the system of record. India is just the mirror."

### 5:45–6:10 · WHAT-IF SIMULATION

🎬 Open the **Simulation Sandbox**. Run the scenario **"Resupply ship cannot arrive."**
🔊 A tension synth. A projection curve draws across the screen with a "shhhhing" sound.
🎙️ "So the station leader asks the question that actually matters: *what if the resupply ship can't reach us?*"
🎬 The results show endurance in days, the **first failure date with its cause**, and the cost in ₹.
🔊 A heavy "DUN" on each result as it lands.
🎙️ "Endurance, down to the day. The first thing that fails, and why. The cost, in rupees. And the simulation never touches live data. It's a sandbox, so you can explore any decision safely before you make it."

### 6:10–6:35 · RESTORE THE LINK: THE SYNC DRAIN ⭐

🎬 Click **Reconnect**. The banner returns to Online and the **sync queue starts draining**.
🔊 **The music builds back up.** Each batch that syncs plays a rising arpeggio tick: *tik-tik-tik-TIK-TIK*.
🎙️ "Link's back. But thirty kilobits can't send everything at once. So HIMADRI prioritises. **Alerts first. Then summaries. Raw telemetry last.**"
🎬 The queue hits zero. Zoom in on the **row-count match**.
🔊 A satisfying "ding" and a bass hit.
🎙️ "And the count matches exactly. **Zero rows lost.**"

### 6:35–6:50 · AGENT + QR (FAST MONTAGE)

🔊 Fast cuts land on the beat.
🎬 Type in the NL agent: *"How many days of fuel do we have at Maitri?"* The answer appears with citations.
🎙️ "Ask it anything in plain language. You get answers grounded in live data, with sources."
🎬 A phone scans a printed **QR equipment passport**, and the asset's full history opens.
🔊 A camera shutter "chk" as the scan completes.
🎙️ "And scan any machine to see its whole life story. It works offline too."

### 6:50–7:00 · CLOSE

🎬 Pull back slowly from the 3D station. Snow falls. The **HIMADRI** logo fades in.
🔊 The music resolves on one final big hit, then only wind.
🎙️ *(slow, confident)* "Five thousand kilometres of ice. Thirty kilobits of hope." *(beat)* "**HIMADRI. The station never goes dark.**"
🎬 End card: *Team Who Let the Bugs Out · SIH 2026 · PS 26060*
🔊 One last buzzer *beep* from the real hardware. Cut to black.

## Sound Design Cheat Sheet

| Moment | Sound | Where to find it |
|---|---|---|
| Cold open | Wind howl, sub heartbeat, dial-up screech, radio static | Pixabay SFX, freesound.org |
| Punchlines (stick, paper) | Record scratch or "bwomp", thumbtack tick | Pixabay SFX |
| Title drop | Riser, then a bass drop | Search "cinematic riser", "sub drop" |
| Main music bed | Dark synth / cinematic electronic | YouTube Audio Library, Pixabay Music: search "dark cinematic synth", "tech trailer" |
| Hardware beats | Tension tone, alarm sting, glitch hit | Search "UI alarm", "glitch hit" |
| **Buzzer moment** | **The REAL buzzer, recorded live** | Your mic, nothing added |
| Cut the link | **Dead silence**, electrical snap, wind | The contrast is what sells it |
| Sync drain | Rising arpeggio ticks, success ding | Search "UI notification rising" |

**The two silences:** before the buzzer (4:10) and at the link cut (5:05). Don't fill them. The drop to nothing is what makes people go "woww".

---

## Recording Tips

- **Warm up the MQ-2 for 2–3 minutes** before recording the gas beat, or the readings will drift.
- Keep the **ADXL335 still for the first 3 seconds after power-on** (it calibrates to zero). Press **Zero ADXL335** before the shake beat.
- Run `pnpm demo:reset` before every take so the seeded state is clean.
- Record the hardware section **as one continuous take** if you can. A single unbroken shot proves it's real.
- Record the VO separately in a quiet room (a closet full of clothes works well), then lay it over the screen capture.
- If the 3D scene lags during recording, the 2D floor-plan fallback exists, but record the 3D version if at all possible.
